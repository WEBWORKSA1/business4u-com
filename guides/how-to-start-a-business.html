How to start a business: the 12-step checklist

From idea to first paying customer, in the order that saves the most money and the most regret.

By the Business4U Editorial Team. Updated November 12, 2026. 12-minute read.

The 12 steps
  1. Pressure-test the idea
  2. Research the market
  3. Run the numbers
  4. Write a one-page plan
  5. Choose a legal structure
  6. Pick and protect a name
  7. Register and get tax numbers
  8. Licences, permits and insurance
  9. Open a business bank account

Most first-time owners do these steps in the wrong order. They register a company and print business cards before they know whether anyone will pay. The checklist below front-loads the cheap, reversible steps (talking to customers, running numbers) and saves the expensive, sticky ones (leases, inventory, hiring) for when you have evidence.

1. Pressure-test the idea

An idea is a guess about what people will pay for. Test the guess before you spend on it. Write one sentence: "I help [specific customer] solve [specific problem] by [your solution]." Then talk to at least ten people who fit that customer description. Ask what they do today, what it costs them in money and time, and what they've already tried. Don't pitch; listen.

Good signs: people describe the problem without prompting, they're already paying for an imperfect fix, or they ask when they can buy. Weak signs: polite enthusiasm with no urgency. If you don't have an idea yet, take the Business Match quiz.

2. Research the market

  • Competitors: list five. Note their prices, reviews and what customers complain about. Complaints are your openings.
  • Demand signals: search volume for your service, local directory listings, marketplace sales rankings, and community groups where your customers ask for recommendations.
  • Pricing range: where you can realistically sit, and why.

3. Run the numbers

Three numbers decide whether the business can work: what it costs to start, what it costs to run each month, and how many sales you need to cover those costs. Use the startup cost calculator for the first two and the break-even calculator for the third. If break-even requires 40 sales a day and you can realistically handle 10, change the price, the costs or the model now, while it's free.

4. Write a one-page plan

Capture the customer, problem, solution, pricing, channels, costs and 90-day milestones on a single page with the plan builder. You'll expand it later if a lender asks; see how to write a business plan.

5. Choose a legal structure

Your structure determines personal liability, how you're taxed and how much paperwork you'll do. Sole proprietorships are simplest but offer no liability protection. LLCs (in the US) and corporations or limited companies (in Canada, the UK, India and elsewhere) separate your personal assets from business debts and claims. Compare them in LLC vs sole proprietorship vs corporation.

6. Pick and protect a name

Shortlist names with the business name generator, then check three things before committing: your state or provincial business registry, your national trademark database, and domain and social handle availability. If you'll trade under a name that isn't your legal or registered company name, you'll usually need to register it as a trade name or DBA.

7. Register the business and get tax numbers

CountryTypical registrations
United StatesState registration for LLCs and corporations; a free Employer Identification Number (EIN) from the IRS; state tax accounts if you sell taxable goods or hire.
CanadaProvincial or federal registration; a Business Number from the CRA, with program accounts for GST/HST, payroll or import as needed.
United KingdomSole traders register with HMRC for Self Assessment; limited companies register with Companies House; VAT registration once turnover passes the threshold.
IndiaUdyam registration for MSMEs; GST registration above the turnover threshold or for certain activities; company or LLP incorporation through the Ministry of Corporate Affairs if applicable.

Requirements change. Confirm with your government's official business portal.

8. Licences, permits and insurance

Most businesses need at least a local business licence; food, childcare, trades, health, transport and alcohol businesses usually need more. Check city, state or provincial, and national requirements. Then insure the risks that could end the business: general liability for most, professional liability for advice-based services, property insurance for equipment and inventory, and workers' compensation once you hire where required.

9. Open a business bank account and set up books

Keep business money separate from day one. It protects your liability shield, makes tax time faster and builds the transaction history lenders want. Choose simple accounting software and follow our bookkeeping basics.

10. Fund the launch

Start with the smallest amount that gets you to your first customers. Common sources: savings, friends and family, microloans, government-backed startup loans, grants and pre-sales. Compare routes in funding options, browse the funding finder, and check your readiness score.

11. Build your online presence

At minimum: a simple website with clear services, prices or a quote form, and contact details; a free Google Business Profile if you serve a local area; and one social channel where your customers already spend time. See how to build a business website.

12. Launch and win the first 10 customers

Your first customers rarely come from ads. They come from your network, from the people you interviewed in step 1, from local partnerships and from showing up where customers gather. Offer a reason to act now, deliver brilliantly, and ask every happy customer for a review and a referral. Then build a repeatable 90-day marketing plan.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Spending on branding, inventory or a lease before validating demand.
  • Underpricing to win the first customers, then struggling to raise prices.
  • Mixing personal and business money.
  • Forgetting to set aside money for taxes from every payment.
  • Trying every marketing channel at once instead of mastering one or two.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need to start a business?

It ranges from a few hundred dollars for a service business run from home to tens of thousands for food or retail. Use the startup cost calculator with your own numbers and include a reserve for the first months.

Can I start a business while employed?

Often, yes. Check your employment contract for non-compete, moonlighting and intellectual property clauses first. See from side hustle to business.

How long does it take?

Registration can take a day to a few weeks. Validating the idea and landing the first customers usually takes one to three months.
