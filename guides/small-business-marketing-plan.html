A 90-day small business marketing plan

Forget doing everything. Pick the right customer, master two channels, and measure what each customer costs. Here's the plan, week by week.

By the Business4U Editorial Team. Updated November 12, 2026. 11-minute read.

Before you spend anything

Define one ideal customer

Write a short profile: who they are, where they are, what triggers them to look for you, what they compare you with, and what makes them choose. "Busy dual-income parents within 10 km who need weekly meal prep and hate planning" gives you something to aim at. "Everyone who eats" does not.

Sharpen your message

Finish this sentence: "We help [customer] get [outcome] without [pain], unlike [alternative]." Use it on your website, profiles and ads.

Set a budget and a target

Decide what you can afford to pay to win a customer. If a customer is worth $560 in gross profit over their lifetime, spending $100 to win one is healthy. Calculate yours with the LTV:CAC calculator.

For most local businesses, the winning pair is Google Business Profile plus referrals. For online stores, it's usually email plus one paid channel. For B2B services, it's referrals plus targeted outreach or content.

The 90-day plan

Days 1 to 30: foundations

  • Claim and complete your Google Business Profile with photos, services, hours and booking link.
  • Fix your website's essentials: clear offer, prices or a quote form, reviews, and a fast mobile experience. See how to build a business website.
  • Ask every past customer for a review. Make it a one-click link.
  • Set up an email signup with a useful incentive, such as a checklist or first-order discount.
  • Install analytics so you know where visitors and enquiries come from.

Days 31 to 60: first channel at full effort

  • Commit to a weekly rhythm, for example two Google posts, five review requests, one email and ten partner outreach messages.
  • Launch a referral offer that rewards both sides.
  • If testing ads, start small with one offer, one audience and a fixed daily budget. Judge results on cost per customer, not clicks.

Days 61 to 90: measure, cut, double down

  • Calculate cost per lead and cost per customer by channel.
  • Cut anything that can't produce a customer below your target cost.
  • Put the savings into the channel that's working, and add a second channel.

The numbers to track weekly

  • New leads or enquiries, by source.
  • Conversion rate from lead to customer.
  • Customer acquisition cost by channel.
  • Reviews gained and average rating.
  • Repeat purchase or retention rate.

Frequently asked questions

How much should a small business spend on marketing?

There's no single right percentage. Work backwards from what a customer is worth and how many you need, and start with what you can afford to test for 90 days.

Should I use AI for marketing content?

It's useful for drafts, ideas and variations, but edit everything for accuracy and your own voice. See AI for small business.

Do I need to be on every social platform?

No. One platform done consistently beats five done poorly.
