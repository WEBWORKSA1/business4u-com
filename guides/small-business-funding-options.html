Small business funding options compared

Every common way to fund a business, what it really costs, what you give up, and which one fits your stage.

By the Business4U Editorial Team. Updated November 12, 2026. 12-minute read.

Start with three questions

  1. How much, and for what? Equipment, inventory, working capital and startup costs each suit different products.
  2. How will it be repaid? Loans need predictable cash flow. If you don't have it yet, look at grants, savings, pre-sales or equity.
  3. What are you willing to give up? Interest, collateral, personal guarantees or ownership.

Matching funding to your stage

Idea or pre-revenue

Lenders mostly lend against cash flow and history, which you don't have yet. Focus on savings, friends and family, pre-sales, microloans and startup loan programs (for example SBA Microloans in the US, Futurpreneur in Canada, Start Up Loans in the UK, Mudra in India), plus grants and pitch competitions. Kiva's crowdfunded 0% loans are also worth a look.

Early revenue (under two years)

Add equipment financing, lines of credit and online lenders, and start building a relationship with a bank or credit union. Keep clean books: six months of bank statements and a current profit and loss statement unlock most applications.

Established (two years or more)

You'll typically qualify for the lowest-cost money: bank term loans and government-backed programs such as the SBA 7(a) or the Canada Small Business Financing Program, plus larger lines of credit.

High-growth startups

If you're building something that could grow very large quickly, angel investors, accelerators and venture capital may fit. Equity is expensive if the business succeeds, so raise only what you need to reach the next milestone.

How to prepare an application

  • Check your funding readiness score and fix the weakest area.
  • Gather bank statements, tax returns, a profit and loss statement, a balance sheet, registration documents and ID.
  • Write a clear use-of-funds statement and repayment plan; see how to write a business plan.
  • Check your personal credit report for errors before you apply.
  • Apply to several lenders within a short window and compare offers side by side.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a business loan with bad credit?

Sometimes, through secured loans, equipment financing, microlenders, community lenders or revenue-based options, usually at higher cost. Improving your score first often saves more than it costs in time.

Do I need collateral?

Not always, but many loans require collateral, a personal guarantee or both. Government guarantees can reduce collateral requirements.

Are there grants to start a business?

Some, but fewer than people expect, and most target specific groups, sectors or innovation. See how to get a small business grant.
