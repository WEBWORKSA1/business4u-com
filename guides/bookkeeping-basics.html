Bookkeeping basics for small business owners

Clean books make taxes painless, loans possible and decisions obvious. Here's the minimum system that works, and the routine that keeps it working.

By the Business4U Editorial Team. Updated November 12, 2026. 10-minute read.

1. Separate business and personal money

Open a business bank account and, if you use one, a business credit card. Run every business transaction through them and nothing personal. This single habit does more for your books than any software.

2. Choose cash or accrual

Cash basis records income when money arrives and expenses when you pay. It's simple and common for very small businesses. Accrual basis records income when you earn it and expenses when you incur them, giving a truer picture when you invoice customers or hold inventory. Tax rules may require one or the other above certain sizes, so check with an accountant.

3. Set up simple categories

Your chart of accounts is just the list of buckets. Keep it short: sales; cost of goods sold; advertising; bank and payment fees; insurance; professional fees; rent; software; supplies; travel; vehicle; wages; taxes and licences; owner contributions and draws.

5. Keep receipts

Photograph receipts as you go and attach them to transactions in your software. Tax authorities generally require you to keep records for several years, often five to seven depending on the country.

6. Invoice promptly and track what you're owed

Send invoices the day work is done using the invoice generator or your software. Review unpaid invoices weekly and follow up as soon as anything is late.

7. Reconcile every month

Reconciling means matching your records to your bank and card statements so the balances agree. It catches missing transactions, duplicates, bank errors and fraud. Do it monthly; it takes minutes when it's routine and days when it's not.

8. Handle sales tax and payroll correctly

If you charge sales tax, GST, HST or VAT, record it separately; it's not your money. Use the sales tax calculator to check amounts. If you have employees, use payroll software or a provider so withholdings and filings happen on time.

9. Read your three financial statements

  • Profit and loss: revenue minus expenses for a period. Are you profitable? Check margins with the profit margin calculator.
  • Balance sheet: what you own and owe at a point in time.
  • Cash flow statement: where cash came from and went. Profitable businesses still fail when cash runs out.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between bookkeeping and accounting?

Bookkeeping records transactions; accounting interprets them, prepares tax returns and advises on strategy.

When should I hire a bookkeeper?

When bookkeeping takes more than a few hours a month, when you add payroll or inventory, or when you keep falling behind.

How much should I set aside for taxes?

It depends on your country, structure and income. Ask an accountant for a percentage and move it to a separate account with every payment.
