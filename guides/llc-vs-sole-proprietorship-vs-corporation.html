LLC vs sole proprietorship vs corporation

Your business structure decides who is on the hook if things go wrong, how profits are taxed, and how much admin you'll do. Here's how to choose.

By the Business4U Editorial Team. Updated November 12, 2026. 10-minute read.

This guide is general information. Tax and legal outcomes depend on your country, state or province and personal situation. Confirm your choice with an accountant or lawyer.

The four questions that decide it

  1. Liability: could the business cause losses or lawsuits larger than you could personally absorb?
  2. Taxes: will profits be high enough that a different tax treatment saves real money?
  3. Money: will you raise investment or bring in co-owners?
  4. Admin: how much paperwork and cost are you willing to handle?

Liability protection can be lost if you mix personal and business money, sign personal guarantees, or act fraudulently. Lenders commonly ask small business owners for personal guarantees regardless of structure.

By country

United States

Options include the sole proprietorship, general partnership, limited liability company (LLC), and corporation. An LLC can elect to be taxed as an S corporation, which can reduce self-employment tax for owners who pay themselves a reasonable salary once profits are meaningful. Venture-backed startups usually form a C corporation, often in Delaware.

Canada

Canada has no LLC. The main choices are the sole proprietorship, partnership, and corporation (federal or provincial). Incorporation provides limited liability and access to lower small business tax rates on retained active business income, but adds annual filings and a separate corporate tax return.

United Kingdom

Most small businesses are sole traders or private limited companies (Ltd), with partnerships and limited liability partnerships (LLPs) for multi-owner professional firms. A limited company pays Corporation Tax on profits, and directors often take a mix of salary and dividends. Companies must file accounts and a confirmation statement with Companies House each year.

India

Options include sole proprietorship, partnership firm, limited liability partnership (LLP), one person company (OPC) and private limited company. Private limited companies are preferred for raising equity; LLPs suit professional partnerships wanting limited liability with lighter compliance.

A simple decision path

  • Testing an idea with low risk and low revenue? Start as a sole proprietor, separate your finances, and insure the obvious risks.
  • Real liability exposure, a physical location, employees or contracts? Form an LLC (US) or incorporate.
  • Profits well above what you need to live on? Ask an accountant whether corporate tax treatment saves money.
  • Raising equity from investors? Incorporate as a corporation or private limited company.
  • Two or more founders? Whatever the structure, sign a written agreement covering ownership, roles, vesting and exits.

Frequently asked questions

Can I change structure later?

Yes. Many owners start as sole proprietors and incorporate once revenue and risk grow. Converting may have tax consequences, so plan it with an accountant.

Does an LLC protect me from everything?

No. You stay personally liable for your own negligence, for debts you personally guarantee and, in some cases, for unpaid payroll taxes.

Do I need a lawyer to incorporate?

Not always. Many people file online themselves or use a formation service. A lawyer is worth it for multi-founder agreements, investment or regulated industries.
