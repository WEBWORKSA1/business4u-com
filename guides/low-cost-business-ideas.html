20 low-cost business ideas you can start this month

Businesses you can launch with skills, a phone and a modest budget, with honest notes on cost, effort and how to land the first customer.

By the Business4U Editorial Team. Updated November 12, 2026. 9-minute read.

"Low-cost" doesn't mean easy. These ideas need little money up front because the main input is your time and skill. That's the trade-off: you're buying the business with effort instead of capital. Startup cost ranges below are rough estimates for a lean launch and vary by location.

Service businesses

IdeaTypical startup costFirst step
Residential or office cleaning$500 to $3,000Price three packages and offer them to neighbours and local offices.
Pet sitting and dog walking$300 to $2,000Get insured, then list on local groups and pet apps.
Handyman and small repairs$1,500 to $8,000Check licensing limits for your area; start with landlords and property managers.
Mobile car detailing$1,500 to $6,000Offer an office-park special: detail cars in the car park while people work.
Home organising$200 to $1,500Document three before-and-after projects for your portfolio.
Personal training$500 to $4,000Get certified and insured; run small outdoor group sessions to build demand.
Tutoring and exam prep$100 to $1,000Pick one subject and grade level; partner with a local school community.

Product and online businesses

How to choose between them

  1. Start from your strengths. Ideas built on skills you already have get to revenue fastest. The Business Match quiz shortlists ideas from your answers.
  2. Check local demand. Search for the service plus your town. Lots of competitors with poor reviews is a good sign.
  3. Do the maths. Use the freelance rate or break-even calculator to see what you'd need to charge and sell.
  4. Pick a scaling path. Service businesses scale by hiring and systems; product businesses by marketing and inventory; digital products by audience.

Frequently asked questions

What's the cheapest business to start?

Service businesses that use skills you already have, such as tutoring, writing, virtual assistance or consulting, can start for under a few hundred dollars.

Do I need to register a low-cost business?

Usually yes, at least as a sole proprietor or with a trade name, and you'll need to report income for tax. Some activities also need local licences. See the launch checklist.

Should I quit my job to start?

Most people are better off starting on the side and switching once the business reliably covers a good share of their costs. See from side hustle to business.
