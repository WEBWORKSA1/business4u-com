How to build a business website that sells

Your website has one job: turn the right visitor into an enquiry or a sale. Here's how to build one that does, without overspending.

By the Business4U Editorial Team. Updated November 12, 2026. 10-minute read.

1. Get the right domain

Short, easy to spell and matching your business name. A .com is still the default in many markets, while a country domain (.ca, .co.uk, .in) signals you're local. Shortlist names with the name generator, then register the domain in your business's name, with auto-renew switched on.

2. Choose a platform

3. Build the essential pages

  • Home: who you help, what you do, and one clear next step, all visible without scrolling.
  • Services or products: with prices or "from" prices where you can.
  • About: real people and credentials build trust.
  • Reviews or case studies.
  • Contact or booking: form, phone, map and hours.
  • Legal: privacy policy and terms; cookie consent where required.

4. Add conversion essentials

  • One primary call to action per page, such as "Book a free quote", repeated at the top and bottom.
  • Short forms; ask only for what you need now.
  • Social proof near every call to action: star ratings, logos, testimonials.
  • Click-to-call on mobile and an instant booking option where possible.
  • A clear answer to "how much?" Hiding prices often costs more enquiries than it saves.

5. Get found: SEO basics

  • One page per main service, each targeting what customers actually search for, such as "emergency plumber Riverside".
  • Unique page titles and meta descriptions.
  • A complete Google Business Profile linked to your site, with consistent name, address and phone everywhere.
  • Helpful content that answers customers' common questions.
  • Submit a sitemap in Google Search Console.

6. Make it fast and mobile-first

Most visitors arrive on phones. Compress images, avoid heavy sliders and autoplay video, and test on a mid-range phone over mobile data. A slow site loses visitors before they see your offer.

7. Measure

Install analytics, track form submissions and calls as conversions, and review monthly: where visitors come from, which pages lead to enquiries, and where people drop off.

Launch checklist

  • Every form tested and delivering to an inbox you monitor.
  • HTTPS enabled; no broken links.
  • Favicon, page titles and social sharing previews set.
  • Privacy policy and terms published.
  • Analytics and Search Console connected; sitemap submitted.
  • Google Business Profile updated with the new link.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a small business website cost?

From a modest monthly fee on a website builder to several thousand dollars for a custom design. Start simple and invest more once the site is producing leads.

Do I need a blog?

Not at first. Helpful service pages and FAQs often do more. Add articles when you can publish consistently.

Should I build it myself?

Builders make DIY realistic for simple sites. Hire help for e-commerce, custom features or when your time is better spent selling.
