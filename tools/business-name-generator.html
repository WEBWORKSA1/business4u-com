How to pick a name that lasts

  1. Easy to say and spell. If you have to spell it out on the phone, word of mouth suffers.
  2. Room to grow. "Riverside Cupcakes" is clear but limiting if you later sell bread or open in a second town.
  3. Distinctive. Generic descriptive names are hard to protect as trademarks and hard to rank in search.
  4. Domain and handles available. Check the .com and your main social platforms before you fall in love.
  5. Legally clear. Search your business registry and trademark databases for the same or similar names in your industry.

Check before you commit

Name ideas from this tool are generated automatically and are not checked against registries or trademarks. Before you register or print anything, search your national trademark office (for example the USPTO in the US, CIPO in Canada, the UK IPO or the WIPO Global Brand Database), your state or provincial business registry, and app stores and social platforms.

Frequently asked questions

Should my business name match my domain exactly?

Ideally, yes. If the exact .com is taken, a short addition such as "get", "hq" or your town is better than a hyphen or an obscure extension.

Can I use a name that another business already uses?

Avoid it, especially in the same industry or region. It risks a trademark dispute and confuses customers.

Do I need to register a trade name?

If you operate under a name that isn't your legal name, many jurisdictions require you to register it (often called a DBA, trade name or business name registration).
