Break-even calculator

Find out how many units you need to sell, and how much revenue you need each month, before your business stops losing money.

How the break-even point works

Your break-even point is the sales volume where total revenue equals total costs. Below it, you lose money. Above it, every sale adds profit. It depends on three numbers: fixed costs (which stay the same whatever you sell), your selling price, and your variable cost per unit (which rises with every sale).

The formula

Break-even units = Fixed costs ÷ (Price per unit − Variable cost per unit)

The part in brackets is the contribution margin: the money each sale contributes toward covering fixed costs. Break-even revenue is simply break-even units multiplied by price. To find the volume for a target profit, add that profit to fixed costs before dividing.

Worked example

A candle maker has $8,000 of fixed monthly costs, sells candles at $50 and spends $20 per candle on wax, jars, packaging and payment fees. Each candle contributes $30. Dividing $8,000 by $30 gives 266.7, so she needs to sell 267 candles a month (about 9 a day) to break even. To earn $4,000 profit on top, she needs ($8,000 + $4,000) ÷ $30 = 400 candles.

Four ways to lower your break-even point

  • Raise the price. A small price increase goes straight into contribution margin and often cuts break-even volume by more than any cost saving.
  • Cut variable costs. Negotiate with suppliers, buy in bulk, or switch to a cheaper payment processor.
  • Turn fixed costs into variable ones. Commission instead of salary, co-working instead of a lease, or pay-as-you-go software.
  • Sell bundles. Bundles raise the average order value without raising fixed costs.
Selling more than one product? Use a weighted average price and variable cost based on your sales mix, or run the calculator for each product line separately.

Frequently asked questions

Should I include my own salary in fixed costs?

Yes. If you need to pay yourself to live, that pay is a fixed cost. Leaving it out makes the business look healthier than it is.

What if my price is lower than my variable cost?

Then you can never break even, because each sale loses money. Raise the price, cut the cost per unit, or rethink the product.

How often should I recalculate?

Whenever prices, rent, wages or supplier costs change, and at least every quarter.
