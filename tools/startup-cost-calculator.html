Startup cost calculator

Add up what it really takes to open the doors and survive the first months, so you raise or save the right amount the first time.

What this calculator adds up

Running out of cash, rather than a bad idea, is what ends many young businesses. A realistic startup budget has three layers:

  1. One-time launch costs: registration, equipment, opening inventory, branding, deposits and professional setup fees.
  2. A reserve for operating losses: monthly costs minus the revenue you realistically expect at first, multiplied by the number of months you want covered.
  3. A contingency buffer: a percentage on top for the costs nobody plans for.

How many months of reserve?

Six months is a sensible default for most service and retail businesses. Choose more if your sales cycle is long (B2B, construction, manufacturing) or if the business needs a lot of upfront inventory. Choose fewer only if you already have paying customers lined up.

Frequently asked questions

Should I include my own living costs?

Yes, in payroll. If the business has to support you, your pay belongs in the monthly costs, otherwise the reserve will be too small.

Can startup costs be deducted from tax?

Many jurisdictions allow some startup and organizational costs to be deducted or amortized. Rules differ by country, so confirm with an accountant.

Where can I get the money?

Savings, friends and family, microloans, government-backed loans, grants and investors. Browse options in the funding finder.
