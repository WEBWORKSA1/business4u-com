LTV:CAC calculator

Find out whether each customer is worth more than it costs to win them, and how many months it takes to earn that cost back.

The two numbers

Customer lifetime value (LTV) is the gross profit a typical customer brings in over the whole relationship: average order value × purchases per year × gross margin × years they stay. Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is your sales and marketing spend for a period divided by the new customers won in that period.

Reading the ratio

LTV:CACWhat it usually means
Below 1:1Each new customer loses money. Fix pricing, retention or the channel.
1:1 to 3:1Profitable, but little room for error or overhead.
3:1 to 5:1Healthy. A common target for growing businesses.
Above 5:1Very efficient. You may be under-spending on growth.

Worked example

An online coffee roaster has an $85 average order, 4 orders per customer per year, a 55% gross margin and customers who stay about 3 years. LTV is $561. It spends $6,000 a month on ads and wins 60 customers, so CAC is $100. The ratio is 5.6:1 and CAC is paid back in about 6.4 months: a strong case for spending more on the channel that produced it.

How to improve the ratio

  • Raise average order value with bundles and minimums for free shipping.
  • Increase purchase frequency with subscriptions and reminders.
  • Extend lifespan with onboarding and loyalty rewards.
  • Lower CAC by moving budget to referral, SEO and email, which compound over time.

Frequently asked questions

Should LTV use revenue or profit?

Gross profit. Revenue-based LTV overstates what you can afford to spend to win a customer.

How do I estimate lifespan for a new business?

Use churn: if 5% of customers leave each month, average lifespan is roughly 1 ÷ 0.05 = 20 months.

Should CAC include salaries?

Yes, include the cost of sales and marketing staff for a fully loaded CAC.
