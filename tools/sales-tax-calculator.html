Sales tax, GST and VAT calculator

Add tax to a price, or pull the tax out of a total that already includes it. Works for US sales tax, Canadian GST, HST and QST, UK and EU VAT, and Indian GST.

Adding tax vs removing tax

Adding tax: total = net price × (1 + rate). Removing tax: net price = total ÷ (1 + rate). A common mistake is to subtract the rate from a tax-inclusive total. Taking 13% off $113 gives $98.31, not the correct $100, because the tax was calculated on the net price, not on the total.

Examples

  • A $100 service in a province with 13% HST: tax $13, total $113.
  • A UK receipt of £120 including 20% VAT: net £100, VAT £20.
  • A US order of $250 at a combined 8.25% rate: tax $20.63, total $270.63.

Things to check before you charge tax

  1. Registration. Many places require you to register once sales pass a threshold, and some require it from your first sale.
  2. Where the customer is. Online sales are often taxed based on the buyer's location.
  3. What you sell. Food, digital products and services can be taxed differently or be exempt.
  4. Combined rates. US rates can stack state, county and city taxes; in Canada, GST may combine with a provincial tax.
Tax rates and rules change and vary by product and location. Confirm the rate with your tax authority or an accountant before invoicing.

Frequently asked questions

Is VAT the same as sales tax?

Both are consumption taxes, but VAT is collected at each stage of the supply chain with credits for tax already paid, while US sales tax is generally collected once at the final sale.

Do I show tax on my invoices?

Usually yes, as a separate line with your tax registration number where required. The invoice generator does this for you.

Can I reclaim tax I pay on business purchases?

In VAT and GST systems, registered businesses can generally claim input tax credits. Ask an accountant how it works where you are.
