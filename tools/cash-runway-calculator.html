Cash runway calculator

Forecast how many months your cash will last at today's burn rate, and see how growth changes the date you run out or turn cash-flow positive.

Burn rate and runway, defined

Net burn is how much more cash goes out than comes in each month. Runway is how many months of that burn your current cash can cover. A simple version is cash ÷ net burn. This calculator goes further: it grows revenue and expenses month by month at the rates you choose, so improving sales actually extend the forecast.

Worked example

A startup has $60,000 in the bank, $9,000 of monthly revenue and $16,000 of monthly expenses, so it burns $7,000 a month. The simple runway is about 8.6 months. If revenue grows 4% a month and expenses 1%, the gap narrows each month and runway stretches to about 10 months, buying extra time to raise money or reach profitability.

What to do with the result

  • Under 6 months: act now. Raising money or arranging a credit line usually takes 2 to 4 months, and lenders prefer borrowers who aren't desperate.
  • 6 to 12 months: pick the two biggest costs you could cut or delay, and set a monthly sales target that would close the gap.
  • 12 months or more: you have room to invest in growth. Re-run the numbers monthly.
Revenue isn't cash until it's collected. If customers pay on 30 or 60-day terms, use the revenue you actually collect each month.

Frequently asked questions

Should I include loan repayments?

Yes. Include anything that leaves your bank account, including debt payments and owner draws.

What growth rate should I use?

Use your average month-on-month growth over the last three to six months, then run a cautious case at half that rate.

How do I extend runway quickly?

Collect overdue invoices, ask for deposits upfront, pause non-essential subscriptions and renegotiate payment terms with suppliers.
