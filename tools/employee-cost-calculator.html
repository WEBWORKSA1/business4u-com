Employee cost calculator

Salary is only part of what a hire costs. Add taxes, benefits and overhead to see the real number before you post the job.

What goes into the fully loaded cost

  • Employer payroll taxes and contributions. In the US these include Social Security, Medicare and unemployment taxes; in Canada, CPP and EI; in the UK, employer National Insurance. Rates and thresholds change, so check your tax authority each year.
  • Benefits such as health and dental cover and retirement matching.
  • Variable pay: bonuses and commission.
  • Equipment, software and training: laptop, licences, onboarding.
  • Workspace overhead: a share of rent, utilities and supplies.

Worked example

A $52,000 salary with 9% employer taxes, $450 a month in benefits, a 3% bonus, $2,500 of equipment and training and 5% overhead comes to roughly $66,300 a year, or about $5,500 a month. That is about 1.27 times salary and roughly $32 per hour worked.

Will the hire pay for itself?

Compare the monthly cost with what the hire unlocks: new revenue they generate, or the value of the hours they free up for you. If you bill $120 an hour and a hire frees 15 hours of your week for billable work, that is up to $7,800 a month of capacity, comfortably above the cost in the example.

Frequently asked questions

What multiple of salary should I budget?

Many small businesses land between about 1.2 and 1.4 times salary, higher where employers fund health insurance.

Do part-time staff cost less per hour?

Not always. Fixed costs such as equipment and onboarding are spread over fewer hours.

Should I use a payroll provider?

For most small employers, yes. Payroll software handles withholding, filings and year-end forms and reduces costly errors.
