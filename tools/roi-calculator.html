ROI calculator

Measure whether a purchase, campaign or project earned its keep, and compare investments that run for different lengths of time.

The formulas

  • ROI = (Value returned − Amount invested) ÷ Amount invested
  • Annualized return = (Value returned ÷ Amount invested)1 ÷ years − 1

Plain ROI ignores time. A 50% return over one year is far better than 50% over five years. The annualized figure puts both on the same footing, so you can compare a new oven, an ad campaign and paying down a loan.

Worked example

A bakery spends $10,000 on a second oven. Over two years the extra capacity brings in $16,500 of additional gross profit. ROI is 65%, and the annualized return is about 28.5% a year, well above the 9% to 12% the owner would pay on a loan. The oven was a good investment.

What to count as "value returned"

  • Extra gross profit (not revenue) that would not have happened without the investment.
  • Costs saved, such as fewer hours or lower material waste.
  • Any resale value of equipment at the end of the period.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good ROI for a small business investment?

At minimum it should beat your cost of capital: the interest rate you would pay to borrow, or what the money could earn elsewhere. Many owners look for payback within two to three years on equipment.

Is ROI the same as profit?

No. Profit is a dollar amount. ROI expresses that profit as a percentage of what you put in.

Can ROI be negative?

Yes, when the value returned is less than the investment.
