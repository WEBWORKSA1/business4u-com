Profit margin calculator

See how much of every dollar of sales you keep at each level: after direct costs, after running costs, and after tax.

Three margins, three different questions

MarginFormulaWhat it tells you
Gross margin(Revenue − COGS) ÷ RevenueWhether your product or service is priced well against what it costs to deliver.
Operating margin(Gross profit − Operating expenses) ÷ RevenueWhether the business model works once rent, wages and marketing are paid.
Net marginNet profit after tax ÷ RevenueWhat you actually keep from every sale.

Worked example

A small design studio bills $120,000 in a year. Freelancer fees and stock assets (its cost of goods sold) come to $54,000, leaving $66,000 gross profit, a 55% gross margin. Rent, software, insurance and marketing cost $38,000, so operating profit is $28,000, or 23.3%. After 21% income tax, net profit is $22,120: an 18.4% net margin.

What is a good profit margin?

It depends heavily on the industry. Grocery and fuel retailers often run on low single-digit net margins and make it up on volume. Software, consulting and professional services can run much higher because the cost of delivering one more unit is small. Compare yourself with businesses of a similar size in your industry rather than with a universal benchmark, and track your own trend quarter by quarter.

How to improve margin fast

  1. Price first. A price rise flows almost entirely to profit. Test it on new customers before existing ones.
  2. Cut the cost of delivery. Renegotiate with your top three suppliers, which usually account for most of COGS.
  3. Drop unprofitable work. Calculate margin by product or client; the bottom 10% often loses money.
  4. Audit subscriptions. Small recurring software charges quietly erode operating margin.

Frequently asked questions

Is profit margin the same as markup?

No. Margin is profit as a share of the selling price; markup is profit as a share of cost. Use the markup and margin calculator to convert between them.

Should owner pay be in operating expenses?

Yes, if you pay yourself a salary. It gives a truer picture of whether the business could survive paying someone else to run it.

What tax rate should I use?

Use your expected effective rate. It varies by country, structure and income. An accountant can confirm it.
